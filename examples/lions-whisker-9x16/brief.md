# The Lion's Whisker

Retell the Ethiopian folk tale of the lion's whisker in our own words. Almaz marries a
widower and moves into his highland farmhouse, but his young son Dawit will not speak
to her or eat the food she cooks. Heartbroken, she climbs to a wise elder and begs for a
love potion. He says he can make one, but only with a whisker from a living lion. Each
day Almaz carries meat down to the ravine where a lion rests, staying a little closer
than the day before, until the lion eats calmly beside her and she gently plucks one
whisker. The elder drops the whisker into his fire: she needs no potion, he says, only
to approach the boy as patiently as she approached the lion. She does, slowly, and the
story ends with Dawit sharing a meal with her at dusk. Tell it as folklore, warm and
quiet, with suspense around the lion.

Use sixteen short, easily translated narration lines of about ten words each. Give each
line a distinct visual moment that live-action footage can show simply. The lion is
always calm: lying, walking slowly or eating, never leaping or attacking.
